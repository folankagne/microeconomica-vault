---
title: "Mechanism Design and Matching — Overview"
---
Two "topics" chapters that share a common shift in perspective: instead of taking a fixed structure of interaction and asking what behavior it produces, we now ask what structure of interaction *produces a desired behavior*. **Mechanism design** (Chapter 17) works this out for a single binary public-decision problem, showing how carefully designed monetary transfers — the Vickrey-Clarke-Groves mechanism — can make truthful reporting a dominant strategy for everyone, generalizing the [second-price auction](../game-theory-basics/economic-applications-of-nash-equilibrium.md) result. **Matching** (Chapter 18) studies a different design question with no money at all: given two populations who each rank members of the other, find a matching method immune to individuals unraveling it — culminating in the Gale-Shapley algorithm, which always produces a *stable* matching and does so optimally for whichever side initiates the proposals. Both chapters connect naturally back to [equilibrium foundations](../equilibrium-foundations/00-overview.md): mechanism design follows up directly on the auction dominant-strategy result from strategic games, and two-sided matching sits right alongside the one-sided housing-market/top-trading-cycles model as another finite, non-monetary allocation problem with a clean algorithmic solution.

Source: Osborne & Rubinstein (2020), Chapters 17–18 (`beyond-lectures`).

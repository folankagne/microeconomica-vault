title: "Preferences and Choice — Overview"
---
The starting point of individual decision theory: model an agent not by her demographics or brain structure, but by a **preference relation** — a ranking over whatever alternatives are on the table (goods, jobs, political candidates, contingent plans). This folder builds that model from the ground up, in the order the course actually teaches it: preferences first (as a *questionnaire* the agent answers consistently), then utility functions as a convenient numerical stand-in for a preference relation, then choice — a *separate* primitive, since actual behavior is what's observable, not the mental state behind it — and the conditions under which choice behavior can be "rationalized" by some preference relation at all.

Corresponds to Lecture 1 and TD1 of Introduction to Microeconomics (M1 Economics & Psychology, PSE/Paris 1), and Osborne & Rubinstein (2020), Chapters 1–2.

title: "Social Choice — Overview"
---
The vault's closing folder turns from individual and market behavior to **collective** decisions: how should society divide output produced with unequal individual productivities (Chapter 19, "Socialism"), and how should a group's members' individual preferences be combined into one social preference or one chosen outcome (Chapter 20, "Aggregating preferences")? Both chapters share a method — the **axiomatic approach** already used for [expected utility](../choice-under-uncertainty/the-expected-utility-theorem.md) and [choice rationalizability](../preferences-and-choice/property-alpha-and-the-rationalizability-theorem.md): state a short list of properties that any reasonable rule "should" satisfy, then characterize *exactly* which rules satisfy all of them at once. In both chapters the answer is a sharp uniqueness or impossibility result — a single rule (socialism) is pinned down by four fairness axioms, and essentially *no* reasonable rule survives Arrow's and Gibbard-Satterthwaite's axioms except a dictatorship.

Source: Osborne & Rubinstein (2020), Chapters 19–20 (`beyond-lectures`).

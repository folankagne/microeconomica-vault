title: "Consumer Theory — Overview"
---
Applies [the preferences-and-choice apparatus](../preferences-and-choice/00-overview.md) to the specific, central case of economic theory: an agent choosing quantities of goods. The set of alternatives becomes $\mathbb{R}^2_+$ (bundles of two goods), and the properties introduced in the general theory — monotonicity, convexity, differentiability — get their standard economic content and diagrams (indifference curves, marginal rate of substitution). Choice problems become **budget sets**, and rationalizability becomes the question of whether observed purchasing behavior is consistent with maximizing *some* preference relation — the weak axiom of revealed preference.

Corresponds to Lecture 2 and Lecture 4 of Introduction to Microeconomics (TD2, TD3), and Osborne & Rubinstein (2020), Chapters 4–5.

title: "Choice Under Uncertainty — Overview"
---
A second application of [the general preferences-and-choice framework](../preferences-and-choice/00-overview.md), parallel to [consumer theory](../consumer-theory/00-overview.md): here the set of alternatives $X$ becomes $L(Z)$, the set of **lotteries** over a prize set $Z$. Two structural properties — continuity and independence — turn out to characterize exactly the preferences representable by an **expected utility** function, the workhorse of decision theory under risk. The chapter closes by confronting the model with the data (the Allais paradox) and formalizing risk aversion as concavity of the Bernoulli utility function.

Corresponds to Lecture 5 and TD4 of Introduction to Microeconomics, and Osborne & Rubinstein (2020), Chapter 3.
